## 1. Meet & Greet

**Standard phone greeting**
Answer all calls with: *"ISO Skin Cancer & Laser Clinic, [Your Name] speaking. How can I help you?"*

**If the patient's name appears on the phone**
If the caller's name is displayed and you recognise them as a patient, greet them by name to make the interaction more personal. For example: *"Hi Tina, this is [Your Name]. How can I help you?"* Use the patient's name whenever appropriate, but make sure you're confident you've identified the correct caller.

**Patients at reception take priority**
If a patient is waiting or approaches the front desk, attend to the patient in front of you first. Allow incoming phone calls to go through to Jess, our Cliniva AI receptionist, while you assist the patient in the clinic.

Priority order:
1. Patients physically present in the clinic
2. Phone calls

Patients who are already in the clinic should always receive your attention before answering an incoming phone call.

**Welcoming patients**
Whenever possible, greet patients by name when they arrive. For example: *"Hi Sarah, welcome."* Using the patient's name helps create a warm, professional and personalised experience.

**Calls intended for another clinic**
Every phone call is an opportunity to provide good service and assist a potential patient. Occasionally, patients may call us believing they've reached another skin cancer clinic, particularly after seeing an online advertisement. Say: *"Sorry, we are not [other clinic]. We are ISO Skin Cancer & Laser Clinic in Upper Mt Gravatt. If you would like help booking a skin check with us, we would be happy to assist you."*

**Full body photography**
Full body photography will commence in January and will cost $50 per visit. If a patient asks about this service, say: *"We will be offering full body photography from January. The cost is $50 per visit. Would you like me to put you down for it?"*

Important: full body photography is booked as a separate appointment from the patient's Full Skin Examination (FSE) with the doctor; it does not replace the doctor's skin check. Clinical photographs of individual moles or lesions can still be taken when required for monitoring.

**New patient identification**
For all new patients, confirm their identity using an appropriate form of identification, such as:
- Medicare card; or
- Driver's licence.

Ensure the patient's details are entered correctly into the system.

## 2. Create New Patient File

**Checking for an existing patient in Best Practice**
1. Go to: BP Premier → File → Open Patient.
2. Search using one of: phone number (recommended, shows on caller ID), full name, date of birth (day and month is often enough), or address.
3. If a match is found: confirm identity by verifying DOB or address, then proceed to book under the existing file.
4. If no match: create a new patient file.

Ask: *"Have you been to our Upper Mt Gravatt clinic before?"* Many patients will say yes because they've seen Dr Jack or Dr Tina at a **different** location (e.g. Complete Care Doctors, Sundoctors). Clarify that the question is specific to the ISO Clinic at Upper Mt Gravatt: if they've only seen the doctor elsewhere, they are new to ISO and need a new file.

Avoiding duplicate patient files matters: it keeps clinical notes accurate, billing consistent, and reminders correctly managed.

**Creating the new patient file (BP steps)**
✏️ Appointment book → select the provider's column → right-click the appointment time → New Appointment → search before creating a new patient → New Patient: first name, last name, phone number (for messages), Address Line 1 & Suburb → Comms Consent → select all → Enable → OK → Save.

**Completing the patient file**
Over the phone, create the patient's file with the necessary information first; the full details can be completed after the phone call. See [Module 4: Records, Pathology & Patient Photos](/modules/records-pathology-photos) for how to complete past history, allergies, skin cancer history, booking warnings, and merging duplicate files.

## 3. Making Bookings in Best Practice (BP)

**Key point**
- Online bookings: the patient chooses their own time; we cannot rearrange or select it for them.
- Phone bookings: we can guide patients toward times that reduce schedule gaps and keep the day running efficiently.

**Important: never reveal internal scheduling.** Never explain internal scheduling or doctor movements, and never say "Let me check with the doctor", as it signals negotiation and invites pressure.

## 4. Booking Efficiency

Fill gaps in the schedule by steering phone bookings toward adjacent open slots rather than leaving gaps. Examples:
- Bookings at 1:00pm and 3:00pm, patient wants afternoon → offer 1:15/1:30/1:45pm before offering 2:00pm or later.
- Bookings at 11:00am and 12:30pm, patient wants lunchtime → offer 11:15/11:30am first; if they want later, offer 12:15pm before 12:30pm. Avoid leaving a big empty block between 11:30 and 12:30.
- Bookings at 9:00am and 10:00am, patient wants morning → offer 9:15/9:30am first rather than 9:30am alone, to tighten the schedule before offering 10:15am.

**When the requested time isn't available, offer the nearest open slot instead of explaining why**, e.g.:
- Gap 10:00am–12:30pm, patient requests 11:45am → *"That time is unavailable, but I can book you in at 12:45pm."*
- Gap 2:00pm–4:30pm, patient requests 3:00pm → *"The 3:00pm spot isn't available. I have 1:30pm or 4:30pm today, or 3:00pm tomorrow."*
- Gap 11:00am–1:30pm, patient requests 12:00pm → *"12:00pm isn't available, but I can offer 1:45pm."*

**Approved responses**
- "That time isn't available, but I can offer you [next available time]."
- "That slot is booked, but I have [alternative time]."
- "The doctor is unavailable at that time. Would you prefer morning or afternoon?"

**Never say**
- Never explain where the doctor is, or mention gaps in the schedule.
- Never say the doctor has gone out or left the clinic.

## 6. Late Arrivals

**In person**
- Keep communication positive, non-blaming, and professional. Never blame the patient or comment on their lateness.
- If they walk in without apologising, simply check them in and say nothing further.
- If they apologise ("Sorry I'm late") respond warmly: *"No worries at all, it works out perfectly!"*

**Staff principles:** never express frustration, never comment on lateness ("You're late," "You missed your time"), never over-explain or justify. Always sound confident and in control, and protect the doctor's schedule without revealing internal movements.

**When a patient calls to say they'll be late**
- If the doctor is still onsite and not planning to go out: *"That may run into the next appointment. We'll need to see the next patient first when you arrive."*
- If the doctor will not be available or the lateness will disrupt the flow: *"We have another patient booked at that time. I can rebook you for another day that suits you."*
- Keep the tone calm and authoritative; no negotiation.

**What the template names mean**
- **(DR) / (Therapist) / (LED):** which type of provider the appointment is with.
- **Green:** the patient has already confirmed the booking; the Green template is a follow-up reminder sent again closer to the appointment date.
- **Orange:** the patient has **not yet confirmed** the booking. If they don't confirm, the appointment is not secured, so follow up until you get a response.
- **PDT** (1Day PDT, 1wk PDT): sent separately from the standard Green/Orange templates because the message needs to tell the patient the treatment itself takes around 3 hours.
- **1wk Routine Skin:** for patients whose appointment was booked well in advance, e.g. 12 months ago at their last visit. The message must state the appointment is for their routine skin cancer check-up, since the patient won't necessarily remember booking it.
- **New Pt Registration Link:** gives new patients a link to complete registration online, reducing paperwork and speeding up intake on busy days; sent when trying to reach patients to reschedule.

## 9. Post-Procedure Protocols

**4 steps after a Full Skin Exam (FSE)**
1. Check if the patient is happy: "How did it go?"
2. Book in for the next FSE: "Let me put you on our 12-monthly/6-monthly list."
3. Payment: "Today is $150, how would you like to pay?"
4. Rebate: tick "Send Online" if paying by phone/Visa; tick "Instant Rebate" if a debit card is available.

**4 steps after a Spot Check**
1. Check if the patient is happy: "How did it go?"
2. Offer follow-ups: "Do you need to come back for a review?"
3. Payment: "Today is $100, how would you like to pay?"
4. Rebate: tick "Send Online" if paying by phone/Visa; tick "Instant Rebate" if a debit card is available.

**4 steps after a Biopsy**
1. Check if the patient is happy: "How did it go?"
2. Advise 7 days for results; we will message or call when results are available.
3. Ensure follow-up: we don't do "no news is good news", so ask the patient to call back next week if they've heard nothing.
4. Payment: $50 out of pocket for 1 biopsy, +$25 for each additional.

**4 steps after an Excision**
1. Give the patient the aftercare instruction sheet.
2. Book a wound review/ROS (Removal of Stitches); ask the doctor when to book it.
3. Payment: excision fees are private fees, no GST, with a Medicare rebate.
4. Rebate: 7 days. Print a receipt for the patient to chase with Medicare.

**4 steps after PDT**
1. Give aftercare instructions with the consent form.
2. Book a 1-month follow-up, or offer any other day of the week.
3. Payment: $700, no GST, per area.
4. Private health rebate: usually no. Only print an invoice if the patient asks about a private health rebate.

**4 steps after Cosmetic Mole Removal (CMR)**
1. Give the aftercare instructions sheet.
2. Book a 1-month follow-up.
3. Payment: $350 + $100 each additional; no extra fee unless not flat (pigmentation costs extra); billed to the GST doctor account.
4. Private health rebate: no, it is cosmetic.
