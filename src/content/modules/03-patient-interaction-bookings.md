## 1. Meet & Greet

**Standard phone greeting:** "ISO Skin Cancer & Laser Clinic, [Your Name] speaking. How can I help you?"

**Managing in-person and phone interactions at the same time**
If a patient is in front of you and you are on a call:
1. Politely place the caller on hold: "ISO Skin Cancer & Laser Clinic, may I please place you on hold?"
2. Press the hold button before turning your attention to the patient in front of you.
3. Pick up the phone again once you've finished serving the patient in front of you.

Note: the patient in front of you always takes priority over phone calls.

**Welcoming patients in the clinic:** whenever possible, greet patients by name as they arrive — it helps create a welcoming, personalised experience.

**Wrong-clinic calls:** occasionally a patient calls thinking we are a different skin cancer clinic (due to our Google Ads). Reply: *"Sorry, we are not [other clinic] — we are ISO Skin Cancer Clinic at Upper Mt Gravatt. If you need our assistance with booking your skin check, let us know."*

**Full body photography:** we do not currently offer this service. If asked, say: *"We take photos for moles that need to be monitored."*

**Every phone call is an opportunity.** For all new patients, confirm their identity by checking their Medicare card or driver's licence.

## 2. Create New Patient File

**Checking for an existing patient in Best Practice**
1. Go to: BP Premier → File → Open Patient.
2. Search using one of: phone number (recommended — shows on caller ID), full name, date of birth (day and month is often enough), or address.
3. If a match is found: confirm identity by verifying DOB or address, then proceed to book under the existing file.
4. If no match: create a new patient file.

Ask: *"Have you been to our Upper Mt Gravatt clinic before?"* Many patients will say yes because they've seen Dr Jack or Dr Tina at a **different** location (e.g. Complete Care Doctors, Sundoctors). Clarify that the question is specific to the ISO Clinic at Upper Mt Gravatt — if they've only seen the doctor elsewhere, they are new to ISO and need a new file.

Avoiding duplicate patient files matters: it keeps clinical notes accurate, billing consistent, and reminders correctly managed.

**Creating the new patient file (BP steps)**
Appointment book → select the provider's column → right-click the appointment time → New Appointment → search before creating a new patient → New Patient: first name, last name, phone number (for messages), Address Line 1 & Suburb → Comms Consent → select all → Enable → OK → Save.

**New Patient Registration Form**
- All new patients must complete this form; some may already have submitted it online via isoclinic.com.au/new-patient-form.
- Online submissions arrive in the Outlook inbox: admin@isoclinic.com.au. Download the form and upload it to the patient's file in Best Practice before their consultation.
- BP steps: left-click the patient's name on bookings → View Record → Correspondence In → Add → select the file → no category, name subject "New Patient Registration" → Save.

**Completing the patient file**
- *Past history:* Search → type the item the patient wrote → find in drug class/ingredient/specific product/non-drug/other → optionally add severity and comments → Save.
- *Allergy:* Add → search (e.g. "hypertension") → tick active or inactive → untick everything else (e.g. include in summaries/save as reason for visit) → Save. If none known: View Record → Reactions → tick "Nil known."
- *Any skin cancer related history:* View Record (top of profile) → comment → double-click on-screen comment (e.g. "Melanoma 6mthly check") → Save.
- *Booking warnings:* use Appointment Notes for any warnings (e.g. "Patient Rude"). Use "GG Pending" / "Gift given" / "GG Done" for Google Review gift tracking.
- *Merge duplicate patient files:* BP Premier → View → Patient List (F10) → search patient name → select the file to **keep** → Edit → Merge → search for the profile to merge in.

## 3. Making Bookings in Best Practice (BP)

**Key point**
- Online bookings: the patient chooses their own time — we cannot rearrange or select it for them.
- Phone bookings: we can guide patients toward times that reduce schedule gaps and keep the day running efficiently.

**Important — never reveal internal scheduling.** Never explain internal scheduling or doctor movements, and never say "Let me check with the doctor" — it signals negotiation and invites pressure.

## 4. Booking Efficiency

Fill gaps in the schedule by steering phone bookings toward adjacent open slots rather than leaving gaps. Examples:
- Bookings at 1:00pm and 3:00pm, patient wants afternoon → offer 1:15/1:30/1:45pm before offering 2:00pm or later.
- Bookings at 11:00am and 12:30pm, patient wants lunchtime → offer 11:15/11:30am first; if they want later, offer 12:15pm before 12:30pm. Avoid leaving a big empty block between 11:30 and 12:30.
- Bookings at 9:00am and 10:00am, patient wants morning → offer 9:15/9:30am first rather than 9:30am alone, to tighten the schedule before offering 10:15am.

**When the requested time isn't available, offer the nearest open slot instead of explaining why**, e.g.:
- Gap 10:00am–12:30pm, patient requests 11:45am → *"That time is unavailable, but I can book you in at 12:45pm."*
- Gap 2:00pm–4:30pm, patient requests 3:00pm → *"The 3:00pm spot isn't available. I have 1:30pm or 4:30pm today, or 3:00pm tomorrow."*
- Gap 11:00am–1:30pm, patient requests 12:00pm → *"12:00pm isn't available, but I can offer 1:45pm."*

**Approved responses**
- "That time isn't available, but I can offer you [next available time]."
- "That slot is booked, but I have [alternative time]."
- "The doctor is unavailable at that time — would you prefer morning or afternoon?"

**Never say**
- Never explain where the doctor is, or mention gaps in the schedule.
- Never say the doctor has gone out or left the clinic.

## 6. Late Arrivals

**In person**
- Keep communication positive, non-blaming, and professional. Never blame the patient or comment on their lateness.
- If they walk in without apologising, simply check them in and say nothing further.
- If they apologise ("Sorry I'm late") respond warmly: *"No worries at all, it works out perfectly!"*

**Staff principles:** never express frustration, never comment on lateness ("You're late," "You missed your time"), never over-explain or justify. Always sound confident and in control, and protect the doctor's schedule without revealing internal movements.

**When a patient calls to say they'll be late**
- If the doctor is still onsite and not planning to go out: *"That may run into the next appointment. We'll need to see the next patient first when you arrive."*
- If the doctor will not be available or the lateness will disrupt the flow: *"We have another patient booked at that time. I can rebook you for another day that suits you."*
- Keep the tone calm and authoritative — no negotiation.
